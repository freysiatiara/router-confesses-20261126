# Candidate panel — The Router Confesses

Eight one-liner candidates, head-to-head. All counts are characters.

| # | Chars | Candidate |
|---|-------|-----------|
| C1 | 101 | I heard you blame me for three straight movie nights, so I took tonight's climax off. You're welcome. |
| C2 | 104 | I didn't drop the connection. I fainted when the killer walked in — even I didn't see that twist coming. |
| C3 | 97 | I left before the twist on purpose. Nobody spoils the ending for me, not even the people I serve. |
| C4 | 97 | You only ever notice me when I stop working, so I stopped at the exact second you'd feel it most. |
| C5 | 80 | The script said someone had to die at the climax. I volunteered. You're welcome. |
| C6 | 96 | I paused the movie myself. The acting was so bad I filed a complaint with the streaming service. |
| C7 | 107 | I clocked out mid-climax. My contract says no overtime, and frankly, neither of us was enjoying the sequel. |
| C8 | 90 | I cut the Wi-Fi because the popcorn ran out and I refuse to watch a climax without snacks. |

## Verdict

**Winner: C4 — "Stopped to Be Noticed".**

C4 wins on mechanism: a fragile-ego router that notices it is only ever
noticed when it stops working, so it commits deliberate sabotage at the
exact climax second — the moment it will be felt most. Two beats
(resentment, then a precisely timed strike), a clear confession voice, and
a fresh angle: no buffering joke, no bandwidth joke, no firmware joke.

The runners-up fall short: C1 is a solid grudge but the payoff ("took
tonight's climax off") is softer than C4's timed strike; C2 is charming
but the router fainting makes it a bystander, not the confessor; C3 has a
good defiant voice but "left before the twist" dodges the climax itself;
C5 is punchy but the volunteering-death bit is a generic script joke;
C6 blames the acting, which shifts the confession onto someone else;
C7 leans on a workplace-overtime gag that could fit any appliance; C8 is
a snack joke with the router as a side character in its own confession.
